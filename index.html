The Beast Method

Four moves to a dangerous player

Every game has a structure. So does the way I teach. Each stage below maps to a real phase of the Ruy Lopez — because good chess, like good coaching, follows an order.

1.e4

Foundations

Openings you actually understand, not memorize. We build repertoires around ideas — pawn structures, piece activity, and the plans behind the first ten moves.

1...e5

Calculation

Daily tactics, forcing-move training, and candidate-move discipline. This is where most players plateau — and where most rating points are won.

2.Nf3

Attack

Initiative, sacrifice, and pressure. You'll learn to recognize when a position calls for aggression — and how to calculate it cleanly instead of guessing.

2...Nc6

Conversion

Endgame technique so a winning position stays winning. Rook endings, opposite-color bishops, and the technical conversions that separate 1400s from 2000s.
